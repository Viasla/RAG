Thus, starting in states 1, 2, and 3, the expected times to absorption are 3, 4, and 3, respectively.

From the canonical form,

$$
\mathbf{R} = \begin{array}{cc} & 0 & 4 \\ 1 & 1/2 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 3 & 0 & 1/2 \end{array}.
$$

Hence,

$$
\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{N} \mathbf{R} = \begin{pmatrix} 3/2 & 1 & 1/2 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1/2 & 1 & 3/2 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1/2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 4 \\ 2 & 1/2 \\ 3 & 1/4 & 3/4 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

Here the first row tells us that, starting from state 1, there is probability  $3/4$  of absorption in state 0 and  $1/4$  of absorption in state 4.  $\Box$
