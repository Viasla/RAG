# Examples
Consider the Gambler’s Ruin chain on states ({0,1,dots,N}) where states (0) and (N) are absorbing. The transition matrix (mathbf{Q}) contains probabilities (p) and (q) for moving between the (N-1) transient states. The matrix (mathbf{R}) has non‑zero entries only for transitions from the boundary transient states (1) and (N-1) to the absorbing states (0) and (N). Applying (mathbf{B}=mathbf{N}mathbf{R}) recovers the well‑known absorption probabilities (b_{iN}=rac{i}{N}) when (p=q) or (b_{iN}=rac{(q/p)^i-1}{(q/p)^N-1}) when (p
eq q).
