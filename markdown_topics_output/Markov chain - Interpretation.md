# Interpretation
The frog metaphor clarifies that the chain moves from one state to another with fixed probabilities, just as a frog jumps from lily pad to lily pad. In the weather example the states represent daily weather types and the transition matrix encodes the chance of moving from one type to another. Long‑run behavior is interpreted via powers of $P$; for a regular chain the limiting distribution $(0.4,0.2,0.4)$ does not depend on the initial state, showing that after many steps the system “forgets’’ its starting condition.

The expected payout of the casino equals the expected number of tosses needed to reach the absorbing pattern, because each toss corresponds to a fair bet of one dollar; thus BB=E(T^{B}).

The harmonic function f(i) represents a conserved quantity (e.g., proportion of G genes) along the Markov chain, so its value at an absorbing state gives the absorption probability.

In Penney‑ante the odds p_A/p_B are expressed purely in terms of BB, BA, AA, and AB, showing that the game’s outcome can be read off from the expected absorption times of the associated chain.

The text distinguishes several classes of Markov chains—absorbing, ergodic, irreducible, and regular—indicating that these classifications are central to the study of stochastic processes.
