# Historical Notes
The factorial notation and its combinatorial interpretation appear as early as 300 B.C. in India, and by the eleventh century the general formula n! for counting permutations was well known in Indian and Arab mathematics.

In 1708 de Montmort first published the “hat‑check problem” (a derangement problem) in a probability book, using factorials to express the number of permutations without fixed points.

Stirling published the asymptotic approximation n!∼√{2πn}(n/e)^n in 1730 in Methodus Differentialis, and de Moivre used this for the central limit theorem.

Euclid’s 3rd century proof of the infinitude of primes contains a factor n!+1, showing an early use of factorials in number theory.

The use of factorials in combinatorics became widespread in Europe during the 18th century, largely due to de Montmort and Stirling.
