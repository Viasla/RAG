# Interpretation
The definition expresses that the joint probability of any combination of the events is determined solely by the individual probabilities, with no dependence on each other; knowing that one event has occurred does not affect the likelihood of any other event in the set.

If (E) and (F) are independent, learning that (E) has occurred does not change the likelihood of (F); mathematically, (P(Fmid E)=P(F)).  This mirrors the everyday intuition that two unrelated events do not influence each other’s chances of occurring.

Independence means that learning the outcome of one event does not change the probability distribution of the other event.  In practice, when two events are independent, the occurrence of one neither increases nor decreases the likelihood of the other, allowing simple multiplication to compute joint probabilities.
