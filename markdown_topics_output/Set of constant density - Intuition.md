# Intuition
A set of constant density is the locus of points in the sample space where the joint density function of a pair of random variables takes on the same value; it is the set of outcomes that are equally likely. For a two‑dimensional normal distribution, these loci are the level curves of the density and reflect the shape of the probability mass.

Because the density of a correlated bivariate normal depends on the combination x^2-2ρxy+y^2, the constant‑density curves are concentric ellipses that shrink or stretch depending on the correlation coefficient ρ. When ρ=0 the ellipses reduce to circles, while non‑zero ρ skews them along the line y=x or y=-x.
