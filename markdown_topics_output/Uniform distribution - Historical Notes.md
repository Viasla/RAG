# Historical Notes
The concept of a uniform distribution is introduced early in probability theory as the natural way to assign probabilities when every outcome of an experiment is considered equally likely; for instance, it is used to model a fair coin toss and a fair die roll.

The concept of the uniform distribution appears early in probability theory, being introduced in Chapter 1 as the basic assumption that all outcomes of an experiment are equally likely.  In Chapter 5 the definition is restated for the discrete case and the impossibility of a uniform distribution on an infinite countable set is noted, with a brief comment that continuous uniform densities are used for uncountable sets of finite length such as the interval [0, 1].
