# Proof
1. p determines g: substituting the definition of p into g(t) gives g(t)=∑_{j=1}^{n} e^{t x_j}p(x_j). 2. Conversely, assume g(t) is known. Because p(x_j)>0 for all j and x_1<⋯<x_n, the function g(t) can be written as a finite sum of distinct exponential terms. By analyzing the exponential terms of g(t) (e.g., through its derivatives at t=0 or via analytic continuation), one can recover each exponent x_j and its coefficient p(x_j). Thus g(t) determines the values of the mass points and their probabilities. Hence g is a one‑to‑one mapping between the distribution p and the mgf.
