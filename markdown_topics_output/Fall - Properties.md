# Properties
Every ordering has at least one fall (the mandatory fall after the last position).

The number of rising sequences in a permutation \(\sigma\) equals the number of falls in its inverse \(\sigma^{-1}\).

Falls are additive under concatenation: if a permutation is split into blocks, the total number of falls equals the sum of falls within each block plus a possible fall at each block boundary.

The concept of a fall is used in the analysis of riffle shuffles, where the distribution of falls (or equivalently rising sequences) helps quantify how far a shuffled deck is from randomness.
