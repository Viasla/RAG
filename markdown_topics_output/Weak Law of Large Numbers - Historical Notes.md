# Historical Notes
The Law of Large Numbers is sometimes called the law of averages and was historically introduced to connect the mathematical model of probability with the frequency interpretation of probability.

The version stated in the text is often called the "Weak Law of Large Numbers" to distinguish it from the later "Strong Law of Large Numbers" mentioned as an exercise.

Chebyshev introduced the first quantitative form of the law of large numbers in 1867, proving what is now called the weak law using his inequality; this result appears in the text as “Chebyshev, On Mean Values, J. Math. Pure Appl., vol. 12 (1867)”.

The weak law was later refined and distinguished from the strong law, which guarantees almost‑sure convergence; the text contrasts the weak law with the strong law in Exercises 15 and 16.
