# Intuition
The Gambler’s Ruin problem models a simple random walk with absorbing boundaries: the gambler’s fortune moves up by one dollar with probability (p) and down by one dollar with probability (q=1-p).  If the fortune ever reaches the lower boundary 0, the gambler is ruined; if it reaches the upper boundary (T), the gambler has won the target amount.  Because the walk is unbiased when (p=q=1/2) and biased when (p
eq q), the chance of reaching the upper boundary depends critically on the starting fortune (x) and the relative drift of the walk.  The absorbing Markov chain framework makes this intuition precise by assigning transition probabilities to each state and solving for the absorption probabilities.

The gambler’s ruin problem is a classic example of a one–dimensional random walk with absorbing barriers at $0$ and $M$. Each bet moves the walk one step to the right (win) or left (loss). The probability of eventual ruin is essentially the chance that a biased random walk, starting from position $k$, ever hits $0$ before hitting $M$. The recurrence relation reflects the Markov property: the future depends only on the current capital. Solving the recurrence gives a closed form that shows how bias affects survival; when $p>q$ (advantage to the gambler), the ruin probability decreases exponentially with distance from $M$, while in the fair case the ruin probability is linear in the initial position.
