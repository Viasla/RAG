# Historical Notes
The theory of branching processes is traced back to a problem posed by Francis Galton in the Educational Times in 1873, where he considered a large nation of adult males and the distribution of their male offspring per generation.

In 1966 D. G. Kendall wrote an article “Branching Processes Since 1873” in the Journal of the London Mathematical Society which discusses this historical context.

The theory of branching processes is cited from T. E. Harris's *The Theory of Branching Processes* (Berlin: Springer, 1963), p. 9, as the source of the generating function for the process described in this section.

The text discusses empirical data from Keyfitz as a case study for a geometric branching model, using estimated parameters m = 1.837 and d = 0.324 derived from the data.

The Chain Letter Problem (Example 10.14) provides a real‑world illustration of a branching process in the context of a 12‑generation chain letter, attributed to a 1978 anecdote by Free Huizinga.

The branching process is employed in Example 10.14 to model a chain‑letter scheme, where each participant forwards the letter to a random number of new participants.
