# Properties
All elementary probabilities are equal to \(1/n\) and sum to 1; the distribution is invariant under any permutation of the sample space; it is discrete and symmetric; it cannot be extended to a countably infinite sample space because the series \(\sum_{k=1}^\infty 1/n\) would not converge.

Non‑negativity: m(ω)≥0 for every ω.

Normalization: ∑_{ω∈Ω} m(ω)=1 (total probability equals one).

Equality of weights: For a uniform distribution on a finite Ω, m(ω₁)=m(ω₂) for any ω₁,ω₂∈Ω.

Impossibility on countably infinite Ω: No constant a can satisfy both non‑negativity and normalization simultaneously, proving that a uniform distribution function cannot be defined on a countably infinite sample space.

A uniform distribution assigns equal probability density to all points in the specified domain, so the total probability over the domain equals 1.;When coordinates are chosen independently and uniformly, the joint distribution is the product of the marginal uniform distributions.;For any sub‑set of the domain, the probability equals the measure (length, area, or angle) of the sub‑set divided by the measure of the whole domain.
