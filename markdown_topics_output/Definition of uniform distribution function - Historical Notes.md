# Historical Notes
Cardano’s early probability work was restricted to the uniform distribution function, i.e., to equiprobable outcomes; he described probability as the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes, and this restriction persisted for a long time in the development of probability theory.

The impossibility of a uniform distribution on a countably infinite sample space is noted in many introductory probability texts (see Definition 1.3), often illustrated by the argument that assigning the same probability a to every outcome ω∈Ω forces either the total mass ∑_ω a to be infinite (if a>0) or zero (if a=0), violating the axiom that the total probability must equal 1.

The simulations in the discussion of Bertrand’s paradox repeatedly use the phrase “choose … at random”, which in the context of the textbook implies a uniform distribution over the specified interval.
