# Historical Notes
Bertrand’s paradox, first observed by J. Bertrand, illustrates that different methods of selecting a chord in a circle yield different probability distributions for the chord length.  In the context of Theorem 1.1 this paradox is manifested by the three coordinate‑choice procedures described: random midpoint, random horizontal position, and random angle between endpoints.

Jaynes’ analysis, referenced in the text, concludes that the only probability assignment that is invariant under translations and rotations of the circle is the one corresponding to the second procedure (random horizontal position), which yields the probability ½ for chords longer than √3.  This assignment is sometimes called the “correct” one in the literature.
