# Examples
In the simulation of Bertrand's paradox, a third method selects a chord by fixing one endpoint at \((1,0)\) and choosing \(\alpha\) uniformly from \([0,2\pi]\). The chord length is computed via the law of cosines, yielding \(L=\sqrt{2-2\cos\alpha}\). The probability that \(L>\sqrt{3}\) in this case corresponds to angles \(\alpha\) in the interval \((\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{4\pi}{3})\), which occupies one-third of the full circle, giving a theoretical probability of \(1/3\).
