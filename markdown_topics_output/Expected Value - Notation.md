# Notation
The expected value is denoted by $E(X)$ and is often called the mean; the symbol $\mu$ is used as a short form for $E(X)$.

In the dice example the average gain $\mu$ is computed as $1\cdot\frac16-2\cdot\frac16+3\cdot\frac16-4\cdot\frac16+5\cdot\frac16-6\cdot\frac16$.

The notation $m(x)$ denotes the probability mass function of $X$.

$x$ denotes the probability that Huygens wins when his opponent throws first; $y$ denotes the probability when Huygens throws first.

$p$ denotes the probability that God does not exist in Pascal’s wager.

$E(\cdot)$ denotes the expectation operator, as in $E(XY)=E(X)E(Y)$.

Payoffs such as $-u$, $v$, and $-x$ are used in the expected‑value expressions $p(-u)+(1-p)v$ and $p\cdot0+(1-p)(-x)$.

Probability fractions such as $\frac{31}{36}$, $\frac{6}{36}$, and $\frac{30}{36}$ appear in the dice‑game equations.

$E(X)$ denotes the expected value (or mean) of a random variable $X$. The symbol $\mu$ is often used for $E(X)$. For indicator variables $X_i$, $S_n=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ denotes the total count, and $S_n^*$ denotes the standardized form $(S_n-np)/\sqrt{npq}$.
