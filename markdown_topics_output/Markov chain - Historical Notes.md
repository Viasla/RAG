# Historical Notes
In 1907 A. A. Markov began the study of an important new type of chance process that later became known as a Markov chain, introducing the idea that the outcome of a given experiment can affect the outcome of the next experiment.

The method of using an absorbing Markov chain to compute expected waiting times for patterns such as HTH and HHH dates back to Li’s martingale theorem and was later refined by Guibas and Odlyzko (1981) who related pattern‑avoidance counts to absorption probabilities.

The gambler’s‑ruin and stepping‑stone models, classic examples in probability theory, are presented as absorbing Markov chains in Exercises 33 and 32, illustrating the long‑standing use of absorbing chains in population genetics and random‑walk problems.
