# Historical Notes
The relationship that the variance of the sum of independent random variables equals the sum of their variances is presented as Theorem 6.8, a classical result in probability theory that dates back to the development of the law of large numbers.

The extension to an arbitrary number of independent trials, given as Theorem 6.9, follows by mathematical induction and formalizes the behavior of the mean and variance in an independent trials process, a cornerstone of statistical inference.
