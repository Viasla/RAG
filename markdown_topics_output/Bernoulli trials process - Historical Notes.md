# Historical Notes
The Law of Large Numbers was first proved by the Swiss mathematician James Bernoulli in the fourth part of his work Ars Conjectandi published posthumously in 1713.

Bernoulli provided a long discussion of the meaning of his theorem with many examples, including estimating the proportion of white balls in an urn, the probability of dying of a particular disease, and weather occurrences.

He used the idea of repeatedly drawing a ball from an urn with replacement to estimate the unknown proportion of white balls, showing that with large enough n one can achieve any desired accuracy and reliability.

Bernoulli also reflected on whether increasing the number of observations could eventually exceed any given degree of certainty, noting that with infinite observations the probability would tend toward perfect certainty, implying a fixed ratio in random events.

Chebyshev developed his inequality to prove a general form of the Law of Large Numbers, although the inequality itself appeared earlier in a work by Bienaymé and was long referred to as the Bienaymé-Chebyshev Inequality.
