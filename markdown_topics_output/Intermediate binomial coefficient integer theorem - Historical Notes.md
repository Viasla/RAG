# Historical Notes
The integrality of binomial coefficients dates back to early combinatorial theory and was formalised in the 19th‑century works of mathematicians such as Gauss and Kummer. The specific alternating‑multiplication‑division algorithm is a modern pedagogical refinement that appears in many contemporary probability textbooks (e.g., the referenced Theorem 3.5).

Related results, such as Lucas’ theorem (1878) on binomial coefficients modulo a prime, and Feller’s probabilistic treatments (1968), illustrate the deep arithmetic structure underlying binomial numbers, of which the intermediate‑integer property is a concrete manifestation.
