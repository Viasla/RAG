# Interpretation
The inequality provides an explicit upper bound on the chance that the empirical average differs from the true success probability by more than ε; consequently it yields a lower bound on the probability that the average lies inside the interval (p‑ε, p+ε).

In the Bernoulli example the bound predicts at least 79 % confidence that the average of 100 trials lies between 0.2 and 0.4, and at least 97.9 % confidence for 1 000 trials, illustrating how increasing the number of observations dramatically sharpens the guarantee.

Chebyshev's inequality is intended to provide a general upper bound on the probability that a random variable deviates from its mean by a specified amount, regardless of the distribution shape, as long as variance is finite. In this context, it will give a conservative estimate for the probabilities of the binomial random variable (S) falling within the stated ranges.
