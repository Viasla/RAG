# Examples
Example 10.15: X is uniformly distributed on [0,1] (so M=1).  Its moments are μₙ=1/(n+1), and the MGF is g(t)=(e^t−1)/t, which converges for all real t.  This concrete case illustrates Theorem 10.3: the support is bounded, the series converges everywhere, and differentiating g(t) at 0 reproduces the moments.

Example 10.16 and 10.17 (exponential and normal distributions) illustrate that when the support is unbounded, the series may fail to converge for all t.  Thus Theorem 10.3 highlights the special status of bounded random variables.
