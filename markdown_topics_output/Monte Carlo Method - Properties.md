# Properties
The accuracy of a Monte Carlo estimate improves as the number of simulated trials increases, in accordance with the law of large numbers; however, the variance of the estimate can be large for problems with low probability events, requiring variance reduction techniques.

The random sequences produced by deterministic pseudorandom generators (e.g., the linear congruential method) are not truly random but behave sufficiently like random variables for many simulation purposes when the seed is chosen appropriately.

The method is especially useful for problems where analytical solutions are difficult or impossible, such as neutron transport, complex combinatorial probabilities, or high-dimensional integrals.
