# Examples
Example 10.5: For independent binomial variables $X$ and $Y$ with $p_X(j)=p_Y(j)=\binom{n}{j}p^{j}q^{n-j}$, the MGFs are $g_X(t)=g_Y(t)=(pe^{t}+q)^{n}$. Their sum $Z=X+Y$ has MGF $g_Z(t)=(pe^{t}+q)^{2n}$, which corresponds to a binomial distribution with parameters $2n$ and $p$.

Example 10.6: For independent geometric variables $X$ and $Y$ with $p_X(j)=p_Y(j)=q^{j}p$, the MGFs are $g_X(t)=g_Y(t)=\frac{p}{1-qe^{t}}$. The sum $Z=X+Y$ has MGF $g_Z(t)=\frac{p^{2}}{1-2qe^{t}+q^{2}e^{2t}}$, which is the MGF of a negative‑binomial distribution.
