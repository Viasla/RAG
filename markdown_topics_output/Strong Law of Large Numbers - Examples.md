# Examples
Examples of the weak law include the Bernoulli trials, coin tossing, and die rolling scenarios.  No examples specifically illustrating the strong law are included in this excerpt.

The classical example is tossing a fair coin repeatedly; the proportion of heads converges almost surely to 1/2.

A counterexample sequence constructed by David Maslen: X_i takes values ±i with probability 1/(2i log i) and 0 otherwise; this sequence satisfies the Weak Law but not the Strong Law.

Another example uses a biased coin with probability p of heads; the Strong Law guarantees that the empirical proportion converges almost surely to p, which is used in a sketch of a probabilistic proof of the Weierstrass approximation theorem.
