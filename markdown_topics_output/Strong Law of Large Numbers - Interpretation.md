# Interpretation
The law of large numbers shows that the sample average converges to the expected value with probability approaching one (weak law).  The text acknowledges that the strong law, which asserts almost sure convergence of the sample average to the mean, will be presented later.

SLLN says that for almost every infinite outcome sequence, the long‑run average equals the expected value; thus the event of convergence has probability one.

In practical terms, if we toss a fair coin many times, the observed proportion will be arbitrarily close to 1/2 for all sufficiently large numbers of tosses, with probability 1.

The failure example shows that if the variance of the summands is not controlled (e.g., increases with i), the empirical average may not converge almost surely even though it converges in probability.
