# Interpretation
The inequality provides a worst‑case probabilistic guarantee on how far an empirical average can stray from the true mean.

It is a foundational tool for establishing convergence in probability, underpinning the Law of Large Numbers.

The bounds become tighter as n grows or as ε increases, but they remain conservative compared with exact binomial probabilities.

In practical terms, it tells a gambler that with a large number of trials the chance of a large relative error is small, even though the bound may overestimate this probability.
