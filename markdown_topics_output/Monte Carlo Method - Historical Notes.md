# Historical Notes
The term 'Monte Carlo Method' originated when John von Neumann and Stanislas Ulam suggested that the problem of calculating complex probability‐related physical phenomena be solved by modeling the experiment by chance devices on a computer; von Neumann chose the code name 'Monte Carlo', and since then the method of simulation has been called the Monte Carlo Method.

During the Second World War the physicists at the Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory used a Monte Carlo simulation to estimate how far neutrons travel through various materials, a question that was beyond the reach of purely theoretical calculations.

The concept of simulation has been in practice for centuries: early experiments such as coin tosses, dice throws, and the Buffon needle experiment were essentially simple Monte Carlo simulations carried out manually or with large tables of recorded outcomes.
