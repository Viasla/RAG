# Examples
**Example 11.24 (Maze).**  The ergodic chain of the maze is turned into an absorbing chain by making state 5 (the food location) absorbing.  Computing the fundamental matrix $N$ gives $Nc=(6,5,6,5,5,6,6)^T$, so the mean first passage time from compartment 1 to the food (state 5) is $6$ steps.  By symmetry the same value holds for starting states 3, 7, 9, and it is one step larger for starting states 2, 4, 6, 8.  The example also notes that the expected number of visits to the starting state before absorption is $14/8$ regardless of the starting compartment.

Exercise 1 asks to compute the fundamental matrix Z for the two‑state chain P=[[1/2,1/2],[1/4,3/4]] and then to obtain the mean first passage matrix using Z.  Exercise 2(a) asks for the average time until a strong Ivy‑League football team appears again, i.e., the mean recurrence time for the "strong" state; Exercise 2(b) asks for the average time from a "weak" state until the chain first reaches a "strong" state, a cross‑state mean first passage time.  Exercise 4 requests the mean recurrence time for each state of Example 11.4 with parameters a=0.5 and b=0.75, which is the mean first passage time from a state back to itself.  Exercise 5 shows that for a repeatedly rolled die the mean time between occurrences of a given number is 6, an explicit mean first passage time calculation.  Exercise 7(d) asks for the expected number of steps before reaching Room 5 for the first time when starting in Room 1, a cross‑state mean first passage time in a maze.  Exercise 9(c) asks for the mean first passage matrix of a random walk on a circle with n=5 and p=0.5, and to verify the formula m_{ij}=d(n-d).  Exercise 14 queries whether reversibility implies symmetry of mean first passage times, using the Land of Oz example.  Exercise 17 derives the equilibrium average time until the next occurrence of state i, \bar{m}_i = z_{ii}/w_i, linking the fundamental matrix to mean first passage times.  Exercise 19 presents the identity \sum_j m_{ij} w_j = \sum_j z_{jj}-1 = K, showing a global property of the mean first passage matrix.
