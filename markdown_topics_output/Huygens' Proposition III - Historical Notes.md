# Historical Notes
Huygens' third proposition appears in his 1692 work *Calculating in Games of Chance*, where he extends his earlier approach for symmetric outcomes to situations with unequal probabilities. He introduces the idea that if a gambler has two possible winnings, a or b, with different numbers of ways, p and q, of occurring, then the expected value can still be deduced by constructing an equivalent gamble in which each of the p+q equally likely elementary outcomes results in either a or b, respectively. This demonstrates Huygens' method of reducing general problems to symmetric ones before applying the weighted average.

The third proposition is framed as an extension of the first two: Prop. I handled two outcomes with equal probability; Prop. II handled three outcomes with equal probability. Prop. III tackles the two-outcome case with unequal probabilities, a necessary generalization for practical gambling problems where the chances are not symmetric.
