# Historical Notes
The binomial coefficients as coefficients of (a+b)^n first appeared in Chinese mathematics around 1100, with references to a tabulation system for unlocking binomial coefficients.

Chu Shih‑chieh presented a triangle giving coefficients up to the eighth power in a book written around 1303 (Figure 3.10), although the surviving copy contains an error that later copies correct.

In the West, Tartaglia (1523, Verona) computed the numbers of possible outcomes for n dice, leading to the earliest known appearance of Pascal’s triangle in European mathematics.

The German mathematician Stifel published a table of binomial coefficients in 1554, and Cardano included Pascal’s triangle in his Opus novum of 1570.

Pascal’s interest in binomial numbers grew from his correspondence with Fermat on the problem of points, where he employed the binomial coefficients to express probabilities of game outcomes.
