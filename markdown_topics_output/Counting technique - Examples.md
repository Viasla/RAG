# Examples
Example 3.1: At a restaurant you choose an appetizer (2 options), a main course (3 options), and a dessert (2 options). Because the choice at each stage is independent of the others, the total number of distinct three‑course meals is 2\times3\times2 = 12.

Tree‑diagram illustration: If 80% of customers pick soup and then 50% of those pick meat, the joint probability for the path “soup then meat” is (8/10)\times(1/2)=4/10, showing that the product of stage probabilities gives the probability of a complete outcome; the same multiplication principle underlies the counting of the number of such paths.
