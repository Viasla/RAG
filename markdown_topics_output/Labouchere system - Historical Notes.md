# Historical Notes
The Labouchere system described in Exercise 9 is named after Henry du Pre Labouchere (1831–1912), an English journalist and member of Parliament.

Labouchere attributed the system to Condorcet, a political leader (1743–1794) who was active during the French Revolution and who had an interest in applying probability theory to economics and politics.

Condorcet’s writings include a calculation of the probability that a jury using majority vote will reach a correct decision when each juror has the same probability of deciding correctly; these works provided a wealth of ideas on how probability might be applied to human affairs.
