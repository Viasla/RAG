# Intuition
The distribution function assigns probabilities to each possible outcome of a discrete random experiment, allowing us to understand how likely each result is and to compute probabilities of related events. For instance, knowing that each face of a fair die has probability 1/6 gives intuition that outcomes are equally likely unless stated otherwise, and we can estimate proportions from repeated trials, as demonstrated by coin toss simulations. This reflects the frequency interpretation: the proportion of times an outcome occurs in many trials approaches its assigned probability.

A distribution function can be imagined as a way of distributing a unit of probability mass among all the possible outcomes of an experiment. Each outcome receives a non‑negative weight, and the total of all these weights is exactly one, so that the distribution describes the likelihood of each outcome occurring. This viewpoint makes it clear that a distribution function is simply a probability mass function over a finite or countable sample space.

The cumulative distribution function represents the probability that a random variable X will take a value less than or equal to a particular number x; it accumulates probability mass or density up to x.
