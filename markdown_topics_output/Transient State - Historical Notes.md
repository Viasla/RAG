# Historical Notes
The term “transient state” appears in the study of absorbing Markov chains, introduced formally in Definition 11.2 of the textbook. It was defined as a state that is not absorbing within an absorbing chain, contrasting with absorbing states that cannot be left. The concept grew out of early 20th‑century work on stochastic processes, where researchers needed a way to distinguish states that are eventually left forever from those that trap the process.
