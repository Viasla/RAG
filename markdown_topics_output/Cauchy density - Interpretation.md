# Interpretation
X measures how far along the wall the reflected light ray lands; larger values of X correspond to larger angles \phi; the uniform distribution of \phi translates into a heavy‑tailed distribution for the distance X, which is exactly the Cauchy distribution.

The equality $k_Z(\tau)=k_X(\tau)$ implies that the distributions of $Z$ and $X$ are identical; thus the Cauchy distribution is stable under linear averaging.

Because the second moment diverges, the usual proof of the Law of Large Numbers, which relies on a finite variance, breaks down; consequently the sample mean does not converge to a deterministic value but remains Cauchy‑distributed.

The characteristic function $e^{-|\tau|}$ uniquely determines the Cauchy density, providing a direct way to verify that $f_X(x)=1/[\pi(1+x^2)]$.
