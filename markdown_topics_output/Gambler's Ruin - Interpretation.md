# Interpretation
The function (w_x) represents the gambler’s chance of success under the stated random walk dynamics; it is a harmonic function with respect to the transition matrix because the expected value of (w) after one step equals (w) at the current state.  In the unbiased case, the chance of winning is proportional to the relative position of the starting fortune between the two absorbing barriers, reflecting symmetry.  In a biased game, the factor ((q/p)^x) captures the exponential attenuation of success probability due to the drift toward loss.  The formula for (w_x) thus quantifies how unfavorable odds and the relative wealth difference between gambler and bank influence ruin probability.

These formulas quantify the gambler’s risk: starting nearer to $M$ reduces the chance of ruin, while a biased coin in the gambler’s favor ($p>q$) further lowers the ruin probability, especially for large $M$. The linear result in the fair case reflects that the random walk is recurrent in one dimension: over infinite time the gambler will almost surely hit either boundary, and the chance of hitting $0$ depends only on the relative distance from $M$. The geometric factor $(q/p)^z$ captures how each incremental step away from the upper boundary multiplies the ruin risk by the bias ratio.
