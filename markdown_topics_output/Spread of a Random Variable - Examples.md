# Examples
Consider a Bernoulli random variable $X$ with $P(X=1)=p$, $P(X=0)=1-p$. Its mean is $\mu=p$, and its spread is $\bar{\sigma}=|1-p|p+|0-p|(1-p)=p(1-p)+(1-p)p=2p(1-p)$. If $Y$ is an independent copy of $X$, the spread of $X+Y$ is not $\sqrt{\bar{\sigma}_X^2+\bar{\sigma}_Y^2}$; a direct calculation shows $\bar{\sigma}_{X+Y}=2p(1-p)$ while $\sqrt{\bar{\sigma}_X^2+\bar{\sigma}_Y^2}=\sqrt{2}\,2p(1-p)$, illustrating that the square of the spread does not add for independent sums.
