# Interpretation
A permutation represents a complete reordering of all elements of a set; the requirement of being one‑to‑one and onto ensures that every element appears exactly once in the output ordering.

Counting permutations corresponds to counting the number of distinct ways to arrange $n$ objects, which is given by the factorial $n!$.

A $k$‑permutation is the number of distinct ways to choose an ordered list of $k$ distinct elements from an $n$‑element set; it captures the idea of arranging a subset of the objects.
