# Interpretation
Buffon’s needle provides a geometric method to estimate π: solving P(E)= (2L)/(πW) for π gives π ≈ (2L)/(W·P̂(E)), where P̂(E) is the observed crossing frequency.

The experiment illustrates geometric probability, where probabilities are ratios of areas (or volumes) in a configuration space rather than counts of equally likely outcomes.

Different experimental designs (e.g., varying the way the midpoint is chosen) can lead to different probability assignments, as highlighted by Bertrand’s paradox; the needle problem avoids this ambiguity by using the natural uniform measures on d and θ.
