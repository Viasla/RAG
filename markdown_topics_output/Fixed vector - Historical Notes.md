# Historical Notes
The concept of a fixed vector in a Markov chain appears in the classical theory of stationary distributions; in this text it is introduced as Definition 11.6 and is later identified as a left eigenvector associated with eigenvalue 1, a viewpoint that dates back to the early development of stochastic processes.

Markov’s 1913 analysis of a 20,000‑letter excerpt from Pushkin’s *Eugene Onegin* introduced the first explicit computation of a fixed vector for a two‑state chain representing vowels and consonants; the resulting vector (0.432,0.568) was shown to match the empirical vowel‑consonant frequencies in the novel.

Later textbooks and research papers (e.g., Kemeny and Snell’s treatment of finite Markov chains and Shannon’s work on English text) repeatedly referred to the fixed vector (often called the stationary distribution) as the key long‑run probability vector, emphasizing its role in ergodic theory and information‑theoretic applications.
