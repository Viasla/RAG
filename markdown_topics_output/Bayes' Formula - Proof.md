# Proof
Bayes began by assuming that the unknown probability p is itself uniformly distributed on [0,1] and that the trials are independent Bernoulli trials with success probability p.

He computed the joint likelihood of observing m successes in n trials as x^m(1‑x)^{n‑m} and then used Bayes’ rule to obtain the posterior density for p, leading to the integral expression for the posterior probability of p lying in [a,b).
