# Intuition
When many independent experiments of equal probability are performed, the standardized total (subtract the mean and divide by the standard deviation) tends to distribute symmetrically about zero, with most values lying near the center and progressively fewer at the extremes.  This behaviour is captured by the bell‑shaped normal curve.

The normal distribution offers a convenient continuous approximation to discrete binomial outcomes, allowing for simpler calculation of probabilities for large sample sizes.
