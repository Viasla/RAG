# Historical Notes
Theorem 9.3, referred to as the Approximation Theorem, is presented in Chapter 9 of the text as a discrete analogue of the classical Central Limit Theorem (CLT).  It establishes that for an independent trials process with common distribution function m(x) having mean μ and variance σ², the probability mass function of the sum S_n converges to a normal density divided by the standard deviation √(nσ²) when the greatest common divisor of the differences of the support values equals 1.  The theorem is illustrated with explicit computer‐generated spike graphs for two non‑Bernoulli distributions, and the program CLTIndTrialsLocal is mentioned as an implementation.  The surrounding discussion connects this result to earlier work on the CLT for discrete sums (Theorem 9.4) and notes that Lindeberg’s condition and its sufficiency results (cited from Feller) provide a broader context for the approximation.  Thus the Approximation Theorem sits between the discrete CLT and the more general Lindeberg–Feller theory.
