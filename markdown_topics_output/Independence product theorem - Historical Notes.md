# Historical Notes
The notion of independence was formalized in the text by Definition 4.1, which states that two events E and F are independent if either both have positive probability and satisfy P(E|F)=P(E) and P(F|E)=P(F), or at least one event has probability 0. The subsequent Theorem 4.1 presents an equivalent characterization using the product of probabilities. The proof and examples given illustrate this concept in the context of coin tosses.

The product rule for independent events dates back to the foundational development of probability theory in the 18th and 19th centuries.  Its extension to continuous sample spaces follows naturally from the definition of conditional probability using densities and integrals.  The examples presented here (e.g., the dart game) illustrate the rule in a geometric context, a common pedagogical setting in probability textbooks.
