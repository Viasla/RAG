# Intuition
Conditional probability captures how our degree of belief in an event F should be updated when we learn that another event E has occurred, by restricting the sample space to the outcomes that are compatible with E and renormalising the original probabilities.

Conditional probability can be viewed as the probability of an event F given that another event E has occurred, effectively restricting attention to the part of the sample space where E holds and renormalizing probabilities. The examples of the spinner and dart illustrate this intuition: restricting the uniform distribution to a subinterval or half of the target multiplies the density by a constant to keep the total probability equal to one in the smaller region. The search problem demonstrates how conditioning on having found nothing in earlier drawers causes the probability of finding the letter in the next drawer to rise as more drawers are ruled out, while the probability of finding it in any later drawer falls.
