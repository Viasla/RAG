# Proof
To prove that the recurrence (w_i=pw_{i+1}+qw_{i-1}) holds, observe that from state (i) the gambler either moves to (i+1) with probability (p) or to (i-1) with probability (q).  The probability of eventually winning when starting in (i) is therefore the weighted average of the winning probabilities from the two reachable states.  The boundary conditions (w_0=0) and (w_T=1) encode that a loss or a win are already achieved.  Solving the second‑order linear difference equation with constant coefficients yields, for the unbiased case (p=q=	frac12), the linear solution (w_i=i/T) that satisfies the boundary conditions.  For the biased case (p
eq q), the general solution is of the form (A+B(r)^i) with (r=q/p); applying the boundary conditions gives (w_i=dfrac{(q/p)^i-1}{(q/p)^T-1}).  This expression is the probability that the gambler reaches the upper boundary before the lower one.  The derivation relies solely on the Markov property and the recurrence relations implied by the transition matrix.

Starting from the basic stepwise argument, if the gambler’s current capital is $k$, a single game changes it to $k+1$ with probability $p$ or to $k-1$ with probability $q$. Therefore $q_k=p,q_{k+1}+q,q_{k-1}$. Manipulating this linear recurrence gives $p(q_{k+1}-q_k)=q(q_k-q_{k-1})$, which implies $q_{k+1}-q_k=(q/p)^k(q_1-q_0)$. Summing these differences from $k=0$ to $M-1$ telescopes to $q_M-q_0=-1$, yielding $(q_1-q_0)sum_{k=0}^{M-1}(q/p)^k=-1$. For $p
e q$, the geometric series gives $(q_1-q_0)=-(q/p-1)/((q/p)^M-1)$. Substituting back and summing up to a general $z$ provides $q_z=1-rac{(q/p)^z-1}{(q/p)^M-1}=rac{(q/p)^M-(q/p)^z}{(q/p)^M-1}$. In the fair case $p=q=1/2$, the recurrence simplifies to $q_{k+1}-q_k=q_k-q_{k-1}$, so the differences are constant; from the boundary values $q_0=1$, $q_M=0$, one obtains $q_z=(M-z)/M$.
