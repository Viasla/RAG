# Historical Notes
Bayes’ work on computing probabilities of a hypothesis given evidence originated in his 1763 essay “An Essay Toward Solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances”, which was published three years after his death and was not published during his lifetime.

Bayes was an ordained minister in Tunbridge Wells, was elected to the Royal Society in 1742, but none of his results appeared in print while he lived.

The formula that is now known as Bayes’ formula was not written by Bayes; it was named by Laplace when he studied inverse problems in statistics.

Bayes’ essay and its ideas were later expanded upon by Laplace, Gauss and other mathematicians, leading to the development of Bayesian analysis as a fundamental branch of statistics.
