# Examples
For n = 6, a = 3, let M = (1,0,2,2,0,2). The corresponding a‑shuffle produces the ordering (3, 1, 4, 5, 2, 6). The a‑unshuffle associated with the same M would take the permutation (3, 1, 4, 5, 2, 6) and restore the deck to the identity order (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6) by reversing the labeling process.

In the context of three riffle shuffles (a = 2 applied three times) of a 52‑card deck, a computer simulation shows that the probability that the original top card ends up among the first 26 positions is approximately 0.5002, illustrating the near‑uniform distribution after a small number of shuffles.

After seven riffle shuffles, the same probability is estimated to be 0.5000003, confirming that the deck is essentially random; this empirical observation matches the theoretical variation‑distance bound of about 0.334 for the 7‑riffle shuffle process.
