# Intuition
The weak law asserts that as the number of independent, identically distributed trials grows, the sample average becomes increasingly likely to be close to the true expected value, reflecting the idea that long‑run frequencies converge to theoretical probabilities.

Intuitively, when we repeat a random experiment many times, the empirical average of the outcomes should become close to the theoretical mean; this is the everyday belief that the proportion of heads in many coin tosses will be about one half.

The weak law formalizes this intuition by stating that the probability that the average deviates from the mean by more than any fixed ε becomes arbitrarily small as the number of trials grows.
