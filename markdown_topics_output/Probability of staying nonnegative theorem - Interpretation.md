# Interpretation
The theorem tells us that the likelihood of a symmetric random walk never dipping below zero over an even number of steps is exactly the central binomial coefficient divided by \(2^{2m}\).

Thus, as \(m\) grows, the probability decays like \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi m}}\) (by Stirling’s formula), reflecting the fact that staying non‑negative becomes increasingly rare for long walks.
