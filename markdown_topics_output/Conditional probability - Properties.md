# Properties
The conditional distribution m(\omega|E) assigns zero probability to outcomes outside E and preserves the relative weights of outcomes inside E, ensuring \sum_{\omega\in E} m(\omega|E)=1.

If P(E)>0, the mapping F\mapsto P(F|E) satisfies the axioms of a probability measure on the sigma‑algebra restricted to E.

From the definition, the product rule P(F\cap E)=P(F|E)P(E) follows, and Bayes' theorem P(E|F)=P(F|E)P(E)/P(F) can be derived.

Conditional probabilities are additive over disjoint events: if F_1 and F_2 are disjoint, then P(F_1\cup F_2|E)=P(F_1|E)+P(F_2|E).
