# Historical Notes
De Moivre, in the mid‑18th century, gave the first proof of the Central Limit Theorem, which provides an approximation to binomial sums and leads naturally to the normal distribution.  His proof relied on an early form of Stirling’s factorial approximation, although he did not determine the exact value of the constant √(2π) that appears in the normal density.

The Central Limit Theorem, as established by De Moivre and later refined by others, shows that the sum of many independent Bernoulli trials approaches a normal distribution, providing the theoretical foundation for the normal curve in probability and statistics.
