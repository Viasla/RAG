# Intuition
Theorem 5.1 captures the intuition that a monotone, differentiable transformation of a random variable simply re‑labels the outcomes: the new distribution’s cumulative probability up to a point equals the old distribution’s cumulative probability at the preimage of that point, and the new density is the old density evaluated at the preimage, scaled by the absolute derivative of the inverse transformation.  In the normal case, this explains why shifting and scaling a standard normal simply moves the mean to µ and stretches the spread to σ while preserving the normal shape.

The theorem follows from the change‑of‑variables technique for continuous densities.  When a random variable (X) is transformed by a differentiable, strictly monotone function (g), each (y) in the range of (g) corresponds to exactly one (x) such that (y=g(x)).  The density at (y) is therefore the density at that (x) divided by the absolute value of the derivative of (g) at (x); summing over all such pre‑images yields the general expression.  In the special case (g(x)=x^2), two pre‑images exist for (r>0) ((sqrt r) and (-sqrt r)), each contributing (	frac{1}{2sqrt r}) times the original density, which leads to the explicit form in the statement.
