# Intuition
The variation distance is introduced as a quantitative measure of how far a given shuffling process is from an ideal random process; it captures the total discrepancy between the actual probability distribution of orderings produced by the process and the uniform distribution over all orderings.

Variation distance measures how far a probability distribution is from being uniform by looking at the largest possible discrepancy in the probability assigned to any event. In the card‑shuffling example it tells us how much more likely a particular outcome (such as the Yin suits winning in New‑Age Solitaire) is under the shuffled deck compared to a truly random deck.
