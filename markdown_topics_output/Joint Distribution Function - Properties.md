# Properties
All joint probabilities are non‑negative and sum to 1 over the entire Cartesian product Ω.

Marginal distributions are obtained by summing the joint distribution over the coordinates of the other variables.

Mutual independence of the component variables is equivalent to the joint distribution being the product of the marginal distributions for every n‑tuple of values.

The joint distribution may assign zero probability to certain n‑tuples that violate deterministic relationships among the variables, as illustrated by the Y_i example.

The joint distribution function is uniquely determined by the probabilities of the elementary outcomes of the underlying experiment; conversely, knowing the joint distribution determines the distribution of any measurable function of the component variables.
