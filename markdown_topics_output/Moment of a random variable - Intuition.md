# Intuition
Moments of a random variable provide a systematic way to capture its entire distribution by summarizing all powers of the variable; the first moment equals the mean and the second moment, together with the first, determines the variance. Higher‑order moments convey finer shape information such as skewness and kurtosis, and a complete knowledge of all moments is sufficient to recover the distribution for discrete variables with finite support. This idea contrasts with the fact that two distinct distributions can share the same mean and variance, as demonstrated by the two distributions (p_X) and (p_Y) in the introductory example. The moment concept therefore expands the descriptive power beyond mean and variance alone.

Moments measure the shape characteristics of a distribution: the zeroth moment is always one, the first moment equals the mean, and the second moment minus the square of the first gives the variance. Higher‑order moments capture skewness, kurtosis and other aspects of the distribution’s spread and tail behaviour. By encoding all moments in a single function, the moment generating function offers a convenient way to analyze and compare random variables.
