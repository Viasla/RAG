# Interpretation
The distribution function provides the probability mass for each specific outcome of a discrete experiment.  It allows us to calculate the likelihood of any event expressed in terms of these outcomes by summing the corresponding probabilities.  In simulation, the distribution function dictates the weighting scheme used to generate random outcomes, ensuring that the simulated frequencies match the theoretical probabilities.

F(x) is used to compute probabilities of intervals of X and to compare the discrete distribution of S_n or S_n^* with its normal approximation.

Plotting F(x) for S_n^* and N(x) illustrates how the distribution of standardized sums approaches the normal distribution as sample size grows.
