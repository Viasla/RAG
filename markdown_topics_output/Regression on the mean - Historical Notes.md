# Historical Notes
The concept of regression on the mean was introduced by Sir Francis Galton in the late nineteenth century. Galton calculated the expected height of a son given the height of the father and demonstrated that tall fathers are expected to have sons who are, on average, shorter than themselves, illustrating the phenomenon that extreme measurements tend to move toward the mean in subsequent observations. The idea also applies to other domains, such as academic performance, where students who score very high on one exam are expected to score lower on the next.

The text references Galton’s work in Exercise 21, noting his calculation of the expected value of a son’s height conditioned on a father’s height, thereby providing a historical example of regression on the mean.
