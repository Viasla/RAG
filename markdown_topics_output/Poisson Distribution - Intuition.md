# Intuition
The Poisson distribution describes the number of occurrences of a rare, approximately independent event in a fixed interval of time or space. In the text the events are bomb hits on a small square of London, cars arriving at an intersection, or vehicles arriving at a road fork; each event is assumed to occur randomly and independently with a small probability per unit, leading to a Poisson count.

When many independent Poisson processes are superimposed, the total count remains Poisson (e.g., arrivals from the north and west at an intersection). Likewise, if each event is kept with a fixed probability (the “thinning’’ operation at a fork), the retained events also follow a Poisson law.
