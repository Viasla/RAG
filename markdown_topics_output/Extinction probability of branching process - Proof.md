# Proof
The extinction probability at generation $n$ follows from the recursive relation $d_{k+1}=f(d_k)$, where $f$ is the offspring generating function. Starting from $d_0=0$, repeated application yields $d_{12}$. For the finite distribution with $p_0=0.2$, $p_1=0.5$, $p_2=0.3$, the generating function is $f(z)=0.2+0.5z+0.3z^2$. Iterating this function twelve times gives $d_{12}approx0.599$, matching the simulation result where 12 out of 20 runs died by generation twelve. In the Poisson case with mean $m=1.1$, the generating function is $f(z)=e^{1.1(z-1)}$. Iterating $f$ twelve times yields $d_{12}approx0.768$, implying a survival probability of $0.232$, which accords with the observation that 6 out of 20 simulated runs yielded a profit. These calculations confirm the theoretical extinction probabilities for the specified distributions.
