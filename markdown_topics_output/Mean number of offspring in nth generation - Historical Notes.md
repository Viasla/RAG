# Historical Notes
The study of the mean number of offspring in successive generations originates from Galton’s branching process problems, where the first problem concerned extinction probability and the second problem concerned the distribution of the number $Z_n$ of offspring in the $n$th generation.

Keyfitz’s 1977 analysis of Japanese female fertility data provided concrete empirical estimates of the offspring distribution, illustrating how the mean number of offspring per parent ($m$) can be estimated from real demographic data.
