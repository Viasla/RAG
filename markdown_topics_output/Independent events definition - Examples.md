# Examples
In a sequence of Bernoulli trials, any collection of specific outcomes forms a sequence of mutually independent events; for example, the event that the first toss is heads and the second is tails are independent because the probability of heads then tails equals 1/2 × 1/2.

Example 4.21 illustrates independence in a continuous setting:  let (E) be the event that a dart lands in the upper half of the target ((yge0)) and (F) the event that it lands in the right half ((xge0)).  Calculating (P(Ecap F)) gives the area of the first quadrant, which equals the product of the areas of (E) and (F); hence (P(Ecap F)=P(E)P(F)), establishing independence.
