# Historical Notes
Theorem 10.2 is built upon earlier work on extinction probabilities for branching processes, notably Reverend H. W. Watson’s first incorrect solution, which mistakenly claimed that extinction occurs with probability 1 in all cases due to a miscalculation.

Earlier communication by Bienaymé (1845) anticipated Galton and Watson by 28 years and correctly identified that the extinction probability depends on the mean offspring number, though his detailed reasoning was never fully published.

The text cites Heyde and Seneta’s translation of Bienaymé’s work, noting that when the mean offspring ratio equals or exceeds unity, families may survive with a probability less than one, providing the foundation for the correct solution that Theorem 10.2 presents.
