The Gambler's Ruin problem models a gambler who starts with an initial stake of size $s$ and plays a sequence of independent games against a dealer (or a rich opponent). In each game, the gambler either wins one unit with probability $p$ or loses one unit with probability $q=1-p$. The gambler stops playing once his capital reaches the absorbing level $M$ or hits $0$, which represents ruin. The central quantity of interest is $q_k$, the probability that the gambler is eventually ruined (reaches $0$) given that the initial stake is $k$ units. It is assumed that $q_0=1$ and $q_M=0$. For $1le kle M-1$, $q_k$ satisfies the recurrence $q_k=p,q_{k+1}+q,q_{k-1}$, which can be rewritten as $q_{k+1}-q_k=(q/p)(q_k-q_{k-1})$.
