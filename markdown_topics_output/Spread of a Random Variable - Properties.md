# Properties
$\bar{\sigma}\ge 0$ and $\bar{\sigma}=0$ iff $X$ is almost surely equal to its mean $\mu$.

The square of the spread plays a role analogous to the variance, but unlike variance, $\bar{\sigma}^2$ is not additive for independent random variables; the example in Exercise 19 demonstrates that $\bar{\sigma}_{X+Y}^2\neq\bar{\sigma}_X^2+\bar{\sigma}_Y^2$ in general.

Both the spread and the standard deviation quantify dispersion around the mean, but the spread uses absolute deviations while the standard deviation uses squared deviations, leading to different mathematical properties.
