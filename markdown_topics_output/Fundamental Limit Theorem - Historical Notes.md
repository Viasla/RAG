# Historical Notes
The text does not provide historical context for the Fundamental Limit Theorem; it is presented as Theorem 11.13 within Chapter 11 of the referenced book.

The fundamental limit theorem was first proved by Doeblin in 1937 in his article 'Exposé de la Théorie des Chaînes Simple Constantes de Markov à un Nombre Fini d’États', as cited in the text.  Earlier arguments used matrix inequalities and monotone sequences of bounds, which appear in the first part of the provided text.  A more modern proof using the coupling method is attributed to T. Lindvall in his 1992 book 'Lectures on the Coupling Method', where an explicit bound on the total variation distance is given.  The theorem itself concerns the long‑run behavior of regular Markov chains, which was a central topic in the development of stochastic processes in the early 20th century.
