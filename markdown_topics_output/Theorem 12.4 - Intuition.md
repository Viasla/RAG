# Intuition
The theorem states that the probability that Peter (or equivalently, Player A) is in the lead exactly (2k) times during a game of (2m) tosses is the same as the probability that the last equalization time of a length‑(2m) symmetric random walk occurs at step (2k). This reveals a deep symmetry: the times when the random walk crosses the t‑axis for the final time encode the same distribution as the times when a player leads in a Heads‑or‑Tails game. The arc‑sine law emerges because the density (f(x)=1/(pisqrt{x(1-x)})) governs both the distribution of the last equalization fraction (k/m) and the fraction of time a player is in the lead. Consequently, the most probable numbers of lead times are the extremes (0) or (2m), while the least probable is (m), matching the minimum of the arc‑sine density at (x=	frac12).
