# Interpretation
$g(t)$ packages all moments of $X$; the $k$‑th derivative at $t=0$ gives $\mu_k$ (since $g^{(k)}(0)=\mu_k$).

Through the limit $\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{g'(t)}{g(t)}$ one can recover the largest value in the support of a finite‑range discrete variable, illustrating how $g$ encodes the support itself.

The relationship $h(z)=g(\log z)$ shows that knowledge of $g$ is equivalent to knowledge of the ordinary generating function, which directly yields the probability mass function via Taylor coefficients.

Properties such as shift, scaling, and convolution translate into simple algebraic transformations of $g$, making it a powerful tool for handling sums of independent variables.
