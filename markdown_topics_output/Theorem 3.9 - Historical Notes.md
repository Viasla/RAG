# Historical Notes
Theorem 3.9 is a central result in the theory of riffle shuffles and was first articulated in the combinatorial analysis of a‑shuffles by Bayer and Diaconis, where the relationship between successive shuffles and a single shuffle with multiplied parameters was formalised.

The coding of a‑unshuffles that underlies Theorem 3.9 is introduced earlier in the text and is referenced again in the discussion of riffle shuffles and variation distance.

Theorem 3.9 is employed in later exercises (e.g., Exercise 3) to analyse probabilities of card positions after multiple riffle shuffles.
