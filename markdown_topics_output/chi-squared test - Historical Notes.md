# Historical Notes
The statistician Karl Pearson analyzed a large number of outcomes at certain roulette tables in 1894 and, following earlier experiments with dice and coin tosses, used the results to develop the chi‑squared test as a method for detecting bias in random devices.

The early experiments of Buffon, Weldon, Wolf and others were suggestive and led to important discoveries in probability and statistics, culminating in Pearson’s development of the chi‑squared test.

The text cites Pearson’s 1894 statement urging the closure of gambling salons to enable proper scientific study of chance, which contextualizes the emergence of the chi‑squared test as a rigorous statistical tool.
