# Proof
The proof for the special case where $\mathbf{P}$ has no zero entries proceeds by showing that the difference between the largest and smallest components of $\mathbf{P}^{n}\mathbf{y}$ decreases geometrically. Lemma 11.1 establishes that if $d$ is the smallest entry of $\mathbf{P}$ and $M_{0}\ge m_{0}$ are the max and min of $\mathbf{y}$, then after one multiplication $$M_{1}-m_{1}\le(1-2d)(M_{0}-m_{0}).$$ Applying this repeatedly yields $M_{n}-m_{n}\le(1-2d)^{n}(M_{0}-m_{0})$ so that the range shrinks to zero as $n\to\infty$. Consequently, all components of $\mathbf{P}^{n}\mathbf{y}$ converge to the same limit, proving that each column of $\mathbf{P}^{n}$ tends to a constant vector. The theorem is then extended to general regular chains by an argument in Exercise 5 that removes zero entries without altering the limit. Finally, since the limit matrix $\mathbf{W}$ has identical columns, it is a stochastic matrix with all rows equal to the unique stationary distribution $\mathbf{w}$, and because $\mathbf{P}$ is regular every entry of $\mathbf{w}$ is strictly positive.
