# Examples
In Lazzerini’s 1901 experiment a needle of nominal length 0.8333 W was dropped 3 408 times; it crossed a line 1 808 times, giving an empirical crossing probability 1 808/3 408≈0.5303. Inserting L/W=5/6 and π≈355/113 into Buffon’s formula yields P(E)=2·(5/6)/(π)≈0.5303, confirming the consistency of the data.

A simple Monte‑Carlo simulation that generates 100 000 random (d,θ) pairs and counts the fraction satisfying d ≤ (L/2) sin θ reproduces the theoretical probability (2L)/(πW) to within statistical error.
