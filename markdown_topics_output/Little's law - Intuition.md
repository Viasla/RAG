# Intuition
Little's law links the three fundamental performance measures of a stable queueing system: arrival rate, average number in the system, and average time a customer spends in the system.  It reflects the idea that, over long periods, the number of customers in the system equals the rate at which customers arrive multiplied by the time they spend in the system.  The law holds even when some customers do not wait at all, as it counts the waiting time of all customers, including those who pass immediately.

The law reflects a conservation principle: over a long period the number of customers entering the system must equal the number leaving. Therefore, the product of how often customers arrive and how long they wait equals the average number that are present. The geometric distribution of queue length shows that higher traffic intensity increases both N and T proportionally, as captured by the formulas.
