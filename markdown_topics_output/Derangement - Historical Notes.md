# Historical Notes
The hat‑check problem, also known as the “derangement” problem, first appeared in an early probability book by Pierre R. de Montmort printed in 1708. De Montmort considered a game called Treize where cards are turned over while calling out numbers, and asked for the probability that no card’s number matches the called number, i.e., a permutation with no fixed point.

De Montmort introduced the notation w_n for the number of permutations of n elements with no fixed point and derived a recursion for w_n. His work predates the modern term “derangement” but provides the earliest recorded combinatorial treatment of the problem.

The use of factorials in counting permutations dates back to Euclid’s proof of the infinitude of primes, but the specific “n!” rule for permutations was known in India as early as 300 B.C. and spread to the Arab world by the eleventh century. The derangement problem later became a classic example in the development of the inclusion‑exclusion principle and the early use of the exponential function in probability, as noted by F. N. David.
