# Interpretation
In an a‑shuffle of the identity ordering, the probability of obtaining a particular ordering D depends only on r(D); specifically  P(D)=Σ_{a,r(D)}/a^{n}, where Σ_{a,r}= {n+a-r choose n}.

Consequently, permutations with the same number of rising sequences are equally likely under a random a‑shuffle, while those with different r have different probabilities.

The rising‑sequence count therefore provides the essential statistic that determines the distribution of the riffle‑shuffle process.

The count of rising sequences measures how far a permutation is from being completely ordered; more rising sequences indicate a greater degree of disorder.

In the analysis of riffle shuffles, the distribution of the number of rising sequences after an \(a\)-shuffle quantifies the mixing effect of the shuffle.
