# Historical Notes
The terminology *binomial coefficient* comes from its application to algebra, which is discussed later in this section.

Pascal’s triangle, a famous triangular array of numbers, displays all binomial coefficients and can be constructed using the recurrence relation α(n,j)=α(n-1,j)+α(n-1,j-1).

The binomial numbers as coefficients of $(a + b)^n$ appeared in the works of mathematicians in China around 1100, where references exist to a 'tabulation system for unlocking binomial coefficients'.

Chu Shih‑chieh produced a triangle giving the coefficients up to the eighth power in a book written around 1303 (see Figure 3.10); the original manuscript is lost but copies survive, one of which contains an error where two equal numbers are not equal.

Tartaglia calculated the number of possible ways that $n$ dice might turn up and used a figurate table that gave the answers for $n$ dice; this was the first appearance of Pascal's triangle in the West, with the table published in 1556.  The problem had suggested itself to Tartaglia in 1523 while he was in Verona.

A table for the binomial coefficients was published in 1554 by the German mathematician Stifel (Arithmetica Integra, 1544).

Pascal’s triangle also appears in Cardano’s *Opus novum* of 1570.
