# Proof
By definition the joint density is f_{X,Y}(x,y) = √{1-ρ^2}^{-1} exp(-(x^2-2ρxy+y^2)/(2(1-ρ^2))), up to a normalizing constant. Setting this equal to a fixed value C and solving for the exponent gives x^2 - 2ρxy + y^2 = -2(1-ρ^2) ln(C·√{1-ρ^2}) = D, which is a quadratic form in (x,y). The quadratic form can be diagonalized by a rotation that removes the cross term, showing that the level set is an ellipse centered at the origin. The eigenvalues of the coefficient matrix are 1-ρ and 1+ρ, which correspond to the squared semi‑axes of the ellipse. The parametric equations x = r cosθ/√{2(1-ρ)} + r sinθ/√{2(1+ρ)} and y = r cosθ/√{2(1-ρ)} - r sinθ/√{2(1+ρ)} arise from substituting the rotation that diagonalizes the quadratic form and scaling by the semi‑axis lengths r/√{2(1-ρ)} and r/√{2(1+ρ)} respectively.
