# Proof
The proof proceeds by counting the a‑shuffles of an n‑card deck in two ways. 1) There are a^n such shuffles, because each card can be assigned independently to one of the a stacks, giving a^n possible cut‑interleaving pairs. 2) For each possible ordering π of the deck having r rising sequences, Theorem 3.11 states that exactly \binom{n+a-r}{n} cut‑interleaving pairs yield π when a‑shuffled from the identity ordering.  Since there are A(n,r) orderings with r rising sequences, summing over all r yields the total number of a‑shuffles: \sum_{r=1}^{a}\binom{n+a-r}{n} A(n,r).  Equating this sum with the known count a^n gives Equation (3.5), completing the proof. The recurrence for A(n,a) follows by isolating the term r=a in that sum, and A(n,1)=1 follows from the observation that only the identity ordering has a single rising sequence.
