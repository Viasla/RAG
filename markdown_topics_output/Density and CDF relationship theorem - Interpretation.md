# Interpretation
The theorem confirms that for continuous random variables, the CDF is the integral of the density, and conversely the density can be recovered by differentiating the CDF wherever it is differentiable.

This relationship allows practitioners to compute the density from a more accessible CDF, especially when the CDF is easier to derive analytically.

It also explains why a continuous CDF may have a discontinuous density, as seen in the example (X=U^2) where the density (f_X(x)=rac{1}{2sqrt{x}}) diverges at (0").
