# Statement
The normalized population size $W_n=Z_n/m^n$ in a Galton–Watson branching process converges in distribution as $n	oinfty$ to a non–degenerate random variable $W$ when the mean offspring number $m>1$ and the extinction probability $d<1$; when $m=1$ the process is critical and $W_n$ converges to zero a.s.; and when $m<1$ the process becomes extinct a.s. with probability one.

For the subcritical case $m<1$ the probabilities of having a specific scaled number of individuals satisfy $m^nP(Z_n=[tm^n])	o(1-d)^2e^{-t(1-d)}$ for every $t>0$, and the extinction probability satisfies $P(Z_n=0)	o d$ as $n	oinfty$.

More generally, for a broad class of branching processes, under suitable regularity conditions there exists a deterministic function $k(t)$ such that $m^nP(Z_n=[tm^n])	o k(t)$ for $t>0$ and $P(Z_n=0)	o d$ as $n	oinfty$.
