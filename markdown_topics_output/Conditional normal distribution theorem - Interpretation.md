# Interpretation
The theorem quantifies how observing Y updates our belief about X: the conditional mean shifts linearly with y, weighted by ρ, while the conditional variance shrinks by a factor of 1-ρ^2, reflecting increased certainty when variables are strongly correlated.  In practice, this explains phenomena such as taller parents tending to have slightly shorter children on average when both variables are strongly positively correlated – an instance of regression to the mean.
