# Intuition
A martingale models a “fair” stochastic process: at any point in time the best predictor of the next value, given all past information, is simply the present value. Consequently, there is no systematic way to profit from the process by looking at its history; the game offers no edge to the gambler.

In practical terms, a gambler’s fortune in a fair coin‑toss game does not, on average, increase or decrease after each toss; the expected change is zero, which is the intuitive content of the martingale notion.

The martingale idea is to adjust the representation of a gambler's fortune so that, despite biased odds, the expected value of the adjusted fortune does not change from one play to the next, making the game appear fair at every stage.

Because the adjusted (nominal) fortune is a martingale, the expected final fortune must equal the initial fortune, which allows one to solve for ruin probabilities by equating the two.
