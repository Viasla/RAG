# Examples
In one dimension the walker starts at 0 on a very long street and at each intersection flips a fair coin to decide whether to step to the left or right by one unit.

In two dimensions the walker moves on a grid of streets; at each intersection one of four directions (north, south, east, west) is chosen with equal probability.

In three dimensions the walker moves on a 3‑dimensional grid (a random climber); at each intersection one of six directions (±x, ±y, ±z) is chosen with equal probability.

The text also describes a programming exercise to simulate random walks in one, two, and three dimensions, measuring the times between returns to the starting point in one dimension and the probability of ever returning in the other dimensions.
