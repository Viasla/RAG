# Interpretation
The computed values of $d_{12}$ illustrate that even in a supercritical branching process (where each individual on average produces more than one offspring), the probability that the process will have gone extinct by a finite time can be large. For the chain‑letter model with modest over‑one mean, approximately two‑thirds of the processes will have died out by generation twelve. The difference between the finite and Poisson models reflects how the tail of the offspring distribution affects extinction: the Poisson distribution allows for larger numbers of offspring with small probability, slightly reducing extinction probability by generation twelve compared to the bounded case. These extinction probabilities directly determine expected gains: a higher chance of survival translates into a higher chance of receiving bonuses from later generations.
