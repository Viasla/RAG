# Examples
Example 6.6 computes the expected number of runs in three coin tosses both by grouping outcomes and by summing 1·(1/8)+2·(1/8)+…; the result 2 equals the sum of the expectations of the indicator variables for each possible run, illustrating linearity.

Example 6.7 considers X (indicator of a head) and Y (die face). The expectation of X+Y is obtained by adding E(X)=1/2 and E(Y)=3.5, giving 4, demonstrating linearity even when the variables are independent.

Example 6.8 finds the expected number of fixed points in a random permutation of {a,b,c}. By defining indicator variables X_i for each element being a fixed point, the expectation of the total F= X_1+X_2+X_3 is the sum of the expectations E(X_i)=1/3, yielding E(F)=1. This uses linearity without needing independence of the X_i.
