# Interpretation
m(ω) represents the probability that the random experiment yields the specific outcome ω; in the coin‑toss example, m(n)=1/2ⁿ is the probability that the first head occurs on the nth toss, and the sum over all n gives the certainty that a head eventually appears.

m(ω) represents the probability that the random experiment produces the specific outcome ω.

The sum of the probabilities over all possible outcomes must equal one, reflecting certainty that some outcome occurs.

$F_X(x)$ represents the probability that the outcome of the experiment lies in the interval $(-\infty,x]$, i.e., the accumulated probability up to $x$.

The text remarks that once the distribution function is known, the density function can be obtained by differentiation, showing that $F_X$ encodes the full probabilistic information of $X$.
