# Properties
All coordinates \(X_1,\dots,X_n\) are mutually independent; for any subset the probability of the intersection equals the product of the individual probabilities.

All coordinates share the same marginal distribution; i.e., \(P(X_i=r_k)=p_k\) for every \(i\) and each outcome \(r_k\) in \(R\).

The sample space of the process is the Cartesian product \(R^n\); consequently the number of possible joint outcomes is \(s^n\).

The joint distribution function of the vector \(\mathbf X\) is the product distribution \(m(\omega)=\prod_{j=1}^n m(\omega_j)\).

Because the trials are identically distributed, the marginal distribution of each \(X_i\) coincides with the common distribution \(m_X\).

The construction guarantees that pairwise independence automatically extends to full mutual independence, a property that does not hold for arbitrary collections of events (as noted in the preceding discussion).

For an independent trials process with common mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, $E(S_n)=n\mu$ and $V(S_n)=n\sigma^2$; $E(A_n)=\mu$ and $V(A_n)=\sigma^2/n$; the standardized sum satisfies $E(S_n^*)=0$ and $V(S_n^*)=1$.
