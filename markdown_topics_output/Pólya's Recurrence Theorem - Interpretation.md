# Interpretation
The theorem explains the long‑term behavior of random walks: in one and two dimensions the walker is almost certain to revisit its origin, which implies that two independent walkers in two dimensions will almost surely meet.  In three dimensions, however, there is a non‑zero chance that a walker never returns to the origin, meaning two walkers in three dimensions may fail to meet.  This distinction between recurrence and transience underlies many results in probability theory and statistical physics.
