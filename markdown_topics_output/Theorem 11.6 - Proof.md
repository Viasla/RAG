# Proof
Starting from any transient state (s_i), the probability of eventual absorption in (s_j) satisfies the recursive relation (displaystyle b_{ij}=p_{ij}+sum_k p_{ik}b_{kj}), where the sum extends over all transient states (s_k). Writing this system for all transient states produces the matrix equation (mathbf{B}=mathbf{P}_{	ext{TA}}+mathbf{Q}mathbf{B}), where (mathbf{Q}) is the sub‑matrix of (mathbf{P}) containing transitions among transient states and (mathbf{P}_{	ext{TA}}) is the sub‑matrix of (mathbf{P}) containing transitions from transient to absorbing states, i.e. (mathbf{R}). Rearranging gives ((mathbf{I}-mathbf{Q})mathbf{B}=mathbf{R}). Since (mathbf{I}-mathbf{Q}) is nonsingular for an absorbing chain, its inverse (mathbf{N}=(mathbf{I}-mathbf{Q})^{-1}) exists, and multiplying both sides by (mathbf{N}) yields the desired identity (mathbf{B}=mathbf{N}mathbf{R}).
