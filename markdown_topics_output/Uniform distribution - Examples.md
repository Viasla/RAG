# Examples
1. Coin tossing with sample space \(\Omega=\{HH,HT,TH,TT\}\): each outcome has probability \(\frac{1}{4}\); a coarser sample space \(\overline{\Omega}=\{0,1,2\}\) with \(\bar{m}(0)=\frac{1}{4}\), \(\bar{m}(1)=\frac{1}{2}\), \(\bar{m}(2)=\frac{1}{4}\) is derived from the uniform distribution on the original sample space.

2. Rolling a pair of fair dice with sample space \(\Omega\) of 36 ordered pairs: the uniform distribution assigns each pair probability \(\frac{1}{36}\); events such as a sum of 7 (6 outcomes) and a sum of 11 (2 outcomes) have probabilities \(\frac{1}{6}\) and \(\frac{1}{18}\) respectively.

3. An alternative, non‑uniform distribution on the same three‑element sample space: \(\overline{q}(0)=\overline{q}(1)=\overline{q}(2)=\frac{1}{3}\) is a legitimate probability function but does not reflect the true distribution of outcomes when a fair coin or die is used.
