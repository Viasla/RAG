# Historical Notes
In 1907 A. A. Markov began the study of a new type of chance process now called a Markov chain, where the outcome of one experiment can affect the next.

The notion of a *regular* Markov chain is introduced later in the text when discussing long‑range behavior, illustrating that the concept was developed after the original Markov chain theory.

The concept of a regular Markov chain is introduced as a stronger condition than ergodicity; many books refer to ergodic chains as "irreducible" while regularity requires a power of the transition matrix to be strictly positive.

The text notes that the term "regular" appears in classical treatments of Markov chains and is linked to early theorems such as Theorem 11.7 (convergence of powers of a regular transition matrix) and Theorem 11.8 (uniqueness of the stationary distribution).
