# Examples
Fair‑coin walk on \mathbb{R}^1: each step X_k equals +1 with probability 1/2 (head) and –1 with probability 1/2 (tail); S_n records the excess of heads over tails after n flips.

Two‑dimensional walk on a city grid: the particle starts at a corner of a square lattice and moves north, south, east, or west according to the distribution of X_k, illustrating a random walk in \mathbf{R}^2.

Three‑dimensional walk in a jungle gym: the particle may move in any of six orthogonal directions (left, right, forward, backward, up, down), providing a random walk in \mathbf{R}^3.

A highly simplified Monopoly board with four squares (GO, B, C, etc.) can be modeled as a random walk on a cyclic graph of four states, where each die roll moves the token clockwise by a random number of squares and the payoff function assigns a monetary gain or loss at each landing.
