# Interpretation
The distribution function m(\omega) gives the probability that the random variable takes the value associated with outcome \omega.

Simulating random variables on a computer involves generating random numbers and mapping them to outcomes according to the distribution function.

The Law of Large Numbers suggests that the relative frequency of outcomes converges to the probabilities given by the distribution function.

The random variable maps each elementary outcome to a numerical or categorical value; each subset of the sample space is an event, and the distribution function assigns the probability mass to each outcome.  The probability of any event is the sum of the masses of the outcomes that belong to that event, thereby linking the random variable to the underlying probability measure.
