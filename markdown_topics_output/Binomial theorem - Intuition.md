# Intuition
The binomial theorem expresses the expansion of (a + b)^n as a sum of terms whose coefficients count the ways to choose which of the n factors contribute a b instead of an a, i.e., the number of r‑element subsets of an n‑set.

The triangle of binomial coefficients (Pascal’s triangle) visualizes how each coefficient is the sum of the two directly above it, reflecting the combinatorial identity binom{n}{r}=binom{n‑1}{r‑1}+binom{n‑1}{r}.

Probabilistic interpretations, such as the probability of obtaining exactly r heads in n tosses of a fair coin, give an intuitive meaning to the binomial coefficients as frequencies of equally likely outcomes.
