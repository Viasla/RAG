# Interpretation
Little's law provides a fundamental relationship in queuing theory that applies to any stable system irrespective of arrival or service distributions, provided the mean values exist.  It allows one to compute one of the three key metrics (arrival rate, average number in the system, average waiting time) if the other two are known.  The law is particularly useful in designing and evaluating service systems, as it highlights the trade‑off between system capacity, customer arrival intensity, and expected delays.  When the traffic intensity ρ exceeds 1 the queue becomes unstable and the law indicates that either N or T must diverge, whereas for ρ < 1 the law guarantees finite values for both.

Little's law provides a fundamental balance for any stable queue: the average number of customers present equals the average arrival rate times the average time a customer spends in the system. It applies regardless of the underlying distribution of interarrival and service times, provided the system reaches steady state. In the M/M/1 case it yields explicit formulas that link traffic intensity, arrival and service rates to expected queue length and waiting time.
