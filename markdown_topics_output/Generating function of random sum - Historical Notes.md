# Historical Notes
The composition property of probability generating functions, such as h(z)=g(f(z)) for a random sum S_N, is a classical result in the theory of branching processes and compound distributions, dating back to early 20th‑century work on Galton‑Watson processes.

The generating function for the Poisson distribution, h(z)=e^{m(z-1)}, was first derived by Siméon‑Denis Poisson and later popularized in probability textbooks as a canonical example of an infinitely divisible law.
