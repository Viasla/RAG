# Proof
In a continuous sample space the independence of E and F is defined by P(E|F)=P(E) and P(F|E)=P(F).  From the definition of conditional probability P(E|F)=P(E∩F)/P(F).  Equating this to P(E) gives P(E∩F)=P(E)P(F).  The converse follows by symmetry: if the product relation holds then P(E|F)=P(E∩F)/P(F)=P(E)P(F)/P(F)=P(E).  The same argument applies for discrete sample spaces and is identical for continuous ones because the definitions of conditional probability are the same.  In Example 4.21, the events E (upper half) and F (right half) of a dart landing on a circle are independent because the area of their intersection equals the product of the areas of each, demonstrating the product rule explicitly.
