# Examples
When X is the process of s successive riffle shuffles (equivalently a single 2^s‑shuffle) applied to the identity ordering, the variation distance can be expressed in terms of rising sequences: \[ \|f_X-u\| = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^n A(n,r)\left|\frac{\binom{2^s+n-r}{n}}{2^{ns}}-\frac{1}{n!}\right| , \] where A(n,r) denotes the Eulerian number of permutations of {1,…,n} with r rising sequences. For a 52‑card deck this formula yields the values displayed in Table 3.14 and Figure 3.13 of the source.

The table shows numerical values of the variation distance after a given number of riffle shuffles of a 52‑card deck; after 7 shuffles the distance is about 0.334. In the New‑Age Solitaire game, after 7 riffle shuffles the Yin suits win about 75% of the time, a 25% excess over the 50% expected under a uniform deck, which is close to the theoretical maximum variation distance of 33.4%.
