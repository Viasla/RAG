# Historical Notes
In one of the first systematic studies of the Poisson distribution, von Bortkiewicz (1898) examined the frequency of deaths from cannon‑ball kicks in the Prussian army corps over a 20‑year period and showed that the observed counts were well modelled by a Poisson law.

Feller (see reference 5) applied the Poisson approximation to a wartime data set: the number of flying‑bomb hits in a 24 × 24 grid of squares over London during World War II. He compared the observed frequencies of 0, 1, 2,… hits with the probabilities given by a Poisson model.
