# Properties
The mean and variance of a Poisson(\(\lambda\)) variable are both equal to \(\lambda\).

If \(X\sim\text{Poisson}(\lambda_1)\) and \(Y\sim\text{Poisson}(\lambda_2)\) are independent, then \(X+Y\sim\text{Poisson}(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)\).

Thinning: if each event of a Poisson(\(\lambda\)) process is kept independently with probability \(p\), the resulting count is Poisson(\(p\lambda\)).

The probability of zero occurrences is \(P\{X=0\}=e^{-\lambda}\).

For large \(\lambda\), the Poisson distribution can be approximated by a normal distribution with mean and variance \(\lambda\).
