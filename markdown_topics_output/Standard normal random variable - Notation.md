# Notation
The standard normal variable is denoted by $Z$; its density function by $f_Z(z)$ and its distribution function by $F_Z(z)$.

When a normal variable $X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^{2})$ is expressed in terms of a standard normal, the relation $X = \sigma Z + \mu$ is used, and conversely $Z = (X-\mu)/\sigma$.

In the Box‑Muller construction, $X = \sqrt{-2\log U}\,\cos(2\pi V)$ and $Y = \sqrt{-2\log U}\,\sin(2\pi V)$ are independent standard normal variates.

The standard normal variable is denoted $Z$ or $X$ when the context makes clear that $X\sim N(0,1)$.

Its density may be written $\phi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^{2}/2}$, and the cumulative distribution function is denoted $\Phi(z)$.

In the bivariate case the joint density is written $f_{X,Y}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\exp\{-\frac{x^{2}-2\rho xy+y^{2}}{2(1-\rho^{2})}\}$, with each marginal equal to $\phi(x)$ or $\phi(y)$.
