# Intuition
Two events are independent if knowing that one occurs gives no information about whether the other occurs; in other words, the probability of the other event is unchanged by the occurrence of the first.

Two events are independent when observing one gives no information about whether the other occurs.  Equivalently, the probability of one event remains unchanged whether or not the other event has happened.  Independence captures the idea of two outcomes being unrelated or uncorrelated in a probabilistic sense.

Two events are independent when knowledge that one has occurred gives no additional information about whether the other has occurred.  Consequently, the chance that both occur is simply the product of their separate chances; each event does not influence the other.
