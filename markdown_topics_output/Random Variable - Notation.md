# Notation
X, X_i, Y are commonly used symbols for random variables; m(\omega) denotes the distribution function (probability mass function).

The floor function \lfloor x\rfloor is used in transforming a random real number r to a random integer: \lfloor6r\rfloor+1.

Probability statements are expressed as P(X\leq4)=2/3, indicating that the random variable X is at most 4 with probability 2/3.

Random variable X is denoted by a capital Roman letter.  The sample space is denoted by the Greek letter Ω.  The distribution function is written as m(ω) and gives the probability mass assigned to outcome ω.  The probability of an event E is written P(E) and is computed as ∑_{ω∈E} m(ω).

The phrase 'random variable' appears as a heading on pages 1 and 18, suggesting standard notation such as X, Y, etc. The index also references 'joint random variable' on page 142, indicating a vector or collection of random variables is denoted together.
