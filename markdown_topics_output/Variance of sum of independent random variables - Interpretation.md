# Interpretation
The additive property of variance for independent random variables implies that when multiple random factors contribute independently to an outcome, the total uncertainty grows proportionally to the number of factors. This is crucial for understanding the behavior of sums and averages in stochastic processes, such as estimating the number of riffle shuffles needed to randomize a deck: the variance quantifies how many shuffles are required on average, and its growth with the number of shuffles informs how quickly the process approaches randomness.
