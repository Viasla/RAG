# Properties
• All probabilities m(ωj) are nonnegative and lie between 0 and 1.  • The probabilities sum to 1: ∑_{j=1}^{n} m(ωj)=1.  • For rational probabilities with common denominator n, a physical or computer simulation can realize the distribution by assigning n_i faces to each outcome ωi where m(ωi)=n_i/n.  • The distribution function is central to the Law of Large Numbers, which states that the empirical frequency of an outcome will converge to its assigned probability as the number of trials grows.

m(ω) is always non‑negative for every ω∈Ω. The sum of m(ω) over all ω∈Ω equals one, guaranteeing that total probability is 1. For any event E⊆Ω, its probability is P(E)=Σ_{ω∈E} m(ω). Consequently, the probability of a single outcome is P({ω})=m(ω). The distribution function is defined only when Ω is finite or countably infinite; for uncountable spaces the concept of a probability density function is used instead.

The cumulative distribution function is non-decreasing and can be visualized by a plot of F(x) against x.

In the context of the exercises, the cumulative distribution of S_n^* is expected to converge toward N(x) as n increases, demonstrating the central limit theorem.
