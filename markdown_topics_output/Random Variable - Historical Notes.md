# Historical Notes
The chapter introduces random variables in the study of discrete probability distributions, particularly in modeling classic experiments such as rolling a die and tossing a coin.

The discussion of Pascal and Fermat’s correspondence on the dice game illustrates the early use of random variables to analyze bets involving repeated experiments.

The concept of a random variable is introduced in this book as a way to represent the outcome of a probabilistic experiment; it is a function that assigns a value to each possible outcome.  The idea that each experiment has an associated random variable was presented early in the chapter to motivate the study of discrete probability distributions.

The index references works by Kahneman (page 38) and others that involve studies of randomness and random variables, indicating historical development in the field.

The entries for 'First Fundamental Mystery of Probability' (page 232) suggest a historical perspective on the concept of random variables in probability theory.

References to 'Law of Large Numbers' (pages 307, 316) point to the classical results underpinning the theory of random variables.
