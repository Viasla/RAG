# Historical Notes
The extinction probability of a branching process, denoted by $d$, has been studied in the context of modeling chain letter schemes, where each participant may pass the letter to a random number of new participants. In the examined example (Example 10.14), the process was simulated for twelve generations using two different offspring distributions: a finite distribution with $p_0=0.2$, $p_1=0.5$, and $p_2=0.3$, and a Poisson distribution with mean $m=1.1$. For the finite case, a computational program (Branch) estimated the probability that the process dies out by the twelfth generation to be $d_{12}=0.599$, which matched the empirical result of 12 out of 20 simulated runs going extinct by generation twelve. For the Poisson case, the same program produced a survival probability $1-d_{12}=0.232$, so that $d_{12}=0.768$; this theoretical value agreed with the observation that only 6 out of 20 simulated runs yielded a profit, implying survival beyond generation twelve. These simulations provide practical evidence of how extinction probabilities behave in finite time horizons.
