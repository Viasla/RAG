# Examples
Example 1: Finite distribution with $p_0=0.2$, $p_1=0.5$, $p_2=0.3$ and mean $m=1.1$. The extinction probability by the twelfth generation is $d_{12}=0.599$; consequently the probability of at least one payment from the twelfth generation (i.e., survival beyond that generation) is $1-d_{12}=0.401$. In simulation, 12 of 20 trials died by generation twelve, closely matching the theoretical $d_{12}$. Example 2: Poisson distribution with mean $m=1.1$. The generating function is $f(z)=e^{1.1(z-1)}$. Iterating this function twelve times yields $d_{12}=0.768$, so the survival probability at generation twelve is $1-d_{12}=0.232$. Simulation of 20 trials produced profit in only 6 runs, consistent with the theoretical survival probability. Example 3: In the chain‑letter problem with $p_0>1/2$, the process is subcritical and the extinction probability is $1$, meaning one cannot expect a profit. This is a direct consequence of the general property that if $p_0>1/2$, then $mle1$ and extinction occurs almost surely.
