# Interpretation
Variance measures the average squared distance of outcomes from the mean; it is the second central moment of the distribution and provides a basis for assessing the reliability of the expected value as a predictor.

Because variance is expressed in squared units of the original variable, its square root (the standard deviation) is often used to interpret dispersion in the original units.

In contexts such as shuffling a deck, a small variance of the number of shuffles needed would indicate that most decks become random after roughly the same number of shuffles.

Since variance is the expected squared deviation from the mean, a larger variance indicates that the values of \(X\) are more spread out around \(\mu\), while a variance of zero means \(X\) equals its mean with probability 1.
