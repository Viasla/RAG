# Historical Notes
The cumulative distribution function of a normal random variable cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions; consequently, extensive tables of its values were compiled (see Appendix A) and numerical integration is required for evaluation.

Because the inverse CDF of a normal distribution also lacks a closed form, special simulation methods were developed, notably the Box‑Muller method introduced by G. E. P. Box and M. E. Muller in their 1958 paper "A Note on the Generation of Random Normal Deviates" (Ann. of Math. Stat. 29, pp. 610‑611).

Tables of the standard normal cumulative distribution function (denoted $F_Z$) have historically been used to avoid creating separate tables for every choice of mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$, since any normal CDF can be reduced to the standard normal case by standardization.

In the discussion of physiological data such as heights of fathers and sons, the text assumes that the underlying random variables are described by normal densities and that, after standardising (subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation), the variables follow a standard normal distribution.

The two‑dimensional standard normal density presented for correlated random variables has marginal densities that are each standard normal, illustrating how the standard normal serves as the basic building block for multivariate normal models.
