# Interpretation
The expected value can be interpreted as the long‑run average outcome of an experiment: in the dice simulation the average gain over many plays approaches the weighted sum of outcomes; similarly, the average height of a sample can be viewed as the expected height of a randomly selected individual.

The frequency interpretation of probability underlies this view: as relative frequencies converge to true probabilities, the sample mean converges to $E(X)$.

Although a single finite experiment’s average is not predictable, the law of large numbers (to be proved later) ensures that the sample average will usually be close to $E(X)$ when the experiment is repeated many times.

Expected value serves as a quantitative measure for comparing alternative actions under uncertainty; the action with the larger expected value is deemed preferable, as illustrated by the belief vs. non‑belief comparison and the choice of betting strategies.

In actuarial contexts, the expected value of a terminal annuity across random lifetimes translates mortality data into a fair price for life annuities.

In gambling, expected value indicates whether a betting system is advantageous; a positive expected value implies a player can, on average, profit over many plays.

The expected value provides a single numerical summary of the central tendency of a probability distribution. It is the theoretical average that would be observed over an infinite sequence of independent repetitions of the experiment.
